Artificial Intelligence Domain

Computer Vision

Teaching computers to see, identify, and process images just like the human eye.

The Definition

Computer Vision (CV) is a field of Artificial Intelligence that trains computers to interpret and understand the visual world. Using digital images from cameras and videos, and deep learning models, machines can accurately identify and classify objects — and then react to what they "see."

Input: Pixel Matrix [32x32]

While humans see a "Dog," a computer sees a Matrix of Numbers (Pixels). CV algorithms process these numbers to find edges, textures, and shapes to identify the object.

Key Task: Pattern Recognition Identifying that 4 vertical lines + a tail shape = "Animal".

Real-World Case Studies

1. Autonomous Vehicles (Self-Driving)

The Problem: How can a car drive safely without a human driver controlling the steering and brakes?

The CV Solution: Self-driving cars (like Tesla or Waymo) use 360-degree cameras to perform three critical tasks:

  • ✔ Lane Detection: Keeping the car between road markings.
  • ✔ Object Detection: Identifying pedestrians, cyclists, and other cars.
  • ✔ Sign Recognition: Reading speed limits and traffic lights.

2. Medical Imaging & Diagnostics

The Problem: Doctors often have to scan thousands of X-rays or MRI images, making human fatigue a risk for missing tiny fractures or tumors.

The CV Solution: AI systems can scan medical images with "Super-Human" precision.

  • ● Early Detection: Detecting cancer cells that are invisible to the human eye.
  • ● Precision Surgery: Robotic arms using cameras to guide surgical tools inside the body.

3. Retail: Cashierless Stores

The Problem: Long queues at checkout counters waste time for customers and require many staff members.

The CV Solution: Stores like Amazon Go use ceiling-mounted cameras to track what you pick up.

  • ▶ Virtual Carts: CV identifies the product in your hand and adds it to your digital bill automatically.
  • ▶ Loss Prevention: Monitoring suspicious behavior to prevent shoplifting.

4. Biometrics & Security

The Problem: Traditional passwords can be forgotten or stolen. Security guards cannot monitor 1000 cameras at once.

The CV Solution: Facial recognition identifies key landmarks on a face (distance between eyes, nose shape) to verify identity.

  • ★ Smartphone Unlock: Using your face to secure your private data.
  • ★ Airport Security: Instantly checking passports against live camera feeds.
