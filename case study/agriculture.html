1. What is Precision Agriculture?

In the 10th-grade Computer Science curriculum, Artificial Intelligence in Agriculture (often called Precision Farming) refers to the application of AI and Machine Learning to ensure that crops and soil receive exactly what they need for optimum health and productivity.

The core mission of AI in this field is Resource Optimization. Instead of spraying an entire field with water or pesticides, AI allows farmers to target specific plants. This reduces waste, lowers costs, and protects the environment from excessive chemical use.

"The future of farming isn't just in the soil; it's in the cloud. By listening to what the plants 'say' through data, AI helps us grow more with less."

1. Aerial Surveillance (Drones)

The Context: Farmers with hundreds of acres cannot physically walk the entire field every day to check for problems.

The Case: AI-powered drones fly over fields using Multispectral Imaging. This allows them to see things the human eye cannot—like a heat signature indicating a plant is "thirsty" or a color shift indicating a pest attack. The AI generates a "Yield Map" that tells the farmer exactly where to focus their efforts.

Outcome: Farmers can cover 100 acres in 20 minutes with 98% accuracy in problem detection.

2. "See & Spray" Robotics

The Context: Weeds compete with crops for nutrients. Usually, farmers spray the entire field with weed-killer, which is expensive and bad for the soil.

The Case: Robots equipped with Computer Vision drive through rows of crops. The AI identifies every single plant. If it sees a "Crop," it does nothing. If it sees a "Weed," it fires a tiny, precise burst of herbicide directly onto the weed.

Real-World: This technology (like John Deere's See & Spray) reduces herbicide use by up to 90%.

3. Predictive Weather Analysis

The Context: A sudden rainstorm or drought can destroy a whole year's worth of work if the farmer isn't prepared.

The Case: AI models analyze decades of weather patterns and satellite data to provide Micro-Local Forecasts. Instead of a general "rain today," the AI tells the farmer "heavy rain at your specific farm at 2 PM." This allows the farmer to harvest early or delay planting, saving thousands of dollars in lost crops.
